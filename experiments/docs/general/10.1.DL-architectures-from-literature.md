# DL architectures from literature for our Raman task

Date: `2026-03-06`

## 1) Why this file

This note captures which neural architectures are commonly used in Raman papers, and which 3 we integrated into `src/` for quick testing in our pipeline.

## 2) Literature-backed candidates

### A. 1D ResNet (high-priority baseline)

- Paper context: deep residual network for Raman-based biological identification.
- Source: Nature Communications (2021), bacterial ID from Raman spectra.
- Link: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-26350-w
- Public implementation reference: https://github.com/csho33/bacteria-ID

Why it fits us:

1. Strong local pattern extraction on 1D spectra.
2. Usually more stable than heavy transformers on limited grouped datasets.
3. Good first DL baseline against classical models.

Our model family: `resnet1d`.

### B. RamanNet-like segmented network

- Paper context: lightweight architecture designed specifically for Raman spectra.
- Source: RamanNet paper (Springer, 2021).
- Link: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42452-021-04741-0
- Public implementation reference: https://github.com/jeff-hykin/RamanNet

Why it fits us:

1. Designed for spectral vectors.
2. Parameter-efficient and fast to train.
3. Practical under hackathon time constraints.

Our model family: `ramannet`.

### C. Transformer/RaT-like 1D spectral model

- Paper context: transformer architecture for Raman spectral classification.
- Source: Scientific Reports (2024), RaT model.
- Link: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-72199-1
- Additional implementation-style reference: https://github.com/zshicode/Raman-prediction-of-LAIP-by-deep-learning

Why it fits us:

1. Captures longer-range dependencies across spectral regions.
2. Useful as a contrast to conv-based models.
3. Can be downsized to fit our data/time budget.

Our model family: `spectral_transformer`.

## 5) Selection logic (go/no-go)

Keep DL candidate if:

1. `macro_f1` improves vs current classical baseline/fusion candidate.
2. Effect is stable across split seeds.
3. Runtime remains acceptable for iterative experiments.

Drop DL candidate if:

1. no improvement over noise,
2. high variance by split,
3. training time is too expensive for the gain.
